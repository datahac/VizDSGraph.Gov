# Researcher instructions: Two-session interface evaluation

## Session 1: Establish the project context

Conduct the interview before showing the generated representation. Use the following approximate prompts, seeking clarification without supplying answers. Maintain the semi-structured format. Adjust terminology based on the participant's background.

1. **Establish system scope.** Ask: “What does the AI system do, for whom and in which settings? What data does it use?” 
2. **Identify roles and authority.** Ask: “What is your role? Who develops, uses, funds or oversees the system? Who makes or approves relevant decisions?”
3. **Establish current priorities.** Ask: “Which decision or milestone needs attention now?
4. **Elicit governance considerations.** Ask: “Which RAI concerns or requirements are you considering? Who raised them, and what makes them relevant?” 
5. **Document activities and evidence.** Ask: “What governance work has occurred? Which documents or artefacts support this account?” 
6. **Identify information needs.** Ask: “How do you currently find and interpret governance information? What remains unresolved? Whose input or earlier experience might help?”

## Session 2: Conduct the interface walkthrough

Provide a consistent brief orientation. Ask the participant to share their screen and think aloud. Observe each initial attempt before offering assistance.

1. **Inspect the project representation.** Ask the participant to explain the project view and identify inaccuracies, omissions or unclear distinctions between current and planned activity.
2. **Trace a candidate requirement.** Ask the participant to select a requirement connected to their priority, locate its source and explain its potential relevance and remaining uncertainties.
3. **Examine responsibility.** Ask the participant to inspect a role and associated activity, distinguishing recorded responsibilities from suggestions and identifying what requires confirmation.
4. **Inspect evidence.** Ask the participant to open a linked artefact or source and explain what it documents, its status and what it leaves unresolved.
5. **Assess a comparison.** Ask the participant to inspect an earlier-project suggestion and explain which similarities and differences affect its relevance.
6. **Identify corrections.** Ask the participant to identify information to correct, qualify or leave unresolved. Record the proposed change and the information or judgement needed.
7. **Assess perceived practical value.** Return to the initial priority. Ask: “What, if anything, would you use or discuss with others? What was confusing, unhelpful or difficult to navigate?”
